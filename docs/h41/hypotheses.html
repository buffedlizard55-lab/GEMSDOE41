5 — Shallow (2 m) temperature-probe anomaly axes as conduit evidence

id: H41-E

rank: 1

layers: GDR 1391 '2m Temperature Probes' (external, free, official) + lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif (scarp corroboration) + gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv temperature field

physical_signature: Elongated axis of a shallow thermal anomaly (ridge/valley line of the anomaly grid), i.e. a curvature/ridge transform on the temperature surface, intersected with a lineament

why_it_should_catch_a_missing_fault: A 2 m anomaly is the most direct surface expression of upflow and was the discovery method at Emerson Pass itself (Anderson & Faulds: 'A thermal anomaly was discovered in Emerson Pass by use of 2 m temperature surveys'). Metre-scale probes resolve what a 100 m grid cannot, and an anomaly on unbroken ground requires a conduit that no scarp map shows.

how_it_differs_from_prior_work: Sibling H33-1 used spring CHEMISTRY (point geothermometry) and GEMSDOE28/H38-1 used a MODELLED conductive heat-flow residual. Nobody in the family has run a ridge/curvature transform on the measured 2 m probe grid.

cost: medium (fetch + resample + ridge transform)

expected: highest of the four above-the-fold candidates; the instrument is measured, not modelled

viability: REQUIRES NEW EXTERNAL DATA: GDR 1391 '2m Temperature Probes' archive. Source is free and official: https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391 . Obtainability from this sandbox is NOT verified - the GDR host was not reachable from bash (curl https://gdr.openei.org -> 000), and the record was not retrieved through the browsing path during this session. VERIFY BEFORE PROPOSING AS VIABLE.

6 — Post-2019 rupture anchors as unmapped-fault evidence (2020 Mw 6.5 Mina / Monte Cristo sequence)

id: H41-H

rank: 2

layers: USGS FDSN event service (external, free, official) -> epicentre trace; corroboration from lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif

physical_signature: The spatial envelope and azimuth of a relocated earthquake sequence (a lineament fitted to hypocentres), emitted as a tapered corridor along the rupture azimuth

why_it_should_catch_a_missing_fault: A fault that ruptured in 2020 is a fault, whether or not it is mapped. The 2020 sequence sits inside this footprint on largely unmapped ground and produced mostly sub-5 cm displacements over zones hundreds of metres wide, so it is unlikely to be preserved in the morphological record that surface-mapping campaigns use. That is precisely the profile of a 'new fault not in the USGS database'.

how_it_differs_from_prior_work: The family's H33-E was declared data-blocked after earthquake.usgs.gov returned HTTP 000 from a bash sandbox. THIS SESSION VERIFIED THE DATA IS OBTAINABLE through the browsing path (see evidence/seismicity_2020.json and registry/seismicity_2020.csv). No repository in the family has executed this.

cost: medium (fetch per time window + lineament fit + corridor taper)

expected: high, and qualitatively different from every catalogue-derived prior: it is an instrument reading, not a map

viability: VERIFIED OBTAINABLE 2026-10-05. Endpoint https://earthquake.usgs.gov/fdsnws/event/1/query returned reviewed network solutions inside the footprint (20 events at M >= 4.6 in the 2020 window). No login, no key, public domain (USGS).

status: IMPLEMENTED THIS SESSION, MEASURED, AND SHIPPED AS A VARIANT. The 16 reviewed USGS events were fetched (registry/seismicity_2020.csv), fitted to an envelope (azimuth 101.0 deg, minor-axis sigma 1,919.5 m), and turned into a 11,992 px corridor whose pixels ship as the `h41-rupture2020-union` artifact. The measured result is a NEGATIVE for the corridor-as-truth hypothesis and a POSITIVE for the catalogue-gap observation: 0 of 16 epicentres lie within 300 m of any mapped fault and 11 lie beyond 3 km, so the 2020 sequence ruptured ground the published catalogue does not express. It does not, by itself, identify which pixels to emit.

7 — Second-derivative curvature of the gravity family (concealed faults under basin fill)

id: H41-F

rank: 3

layers: training_features.tif bands 5 (isostatic gravity anomaly slope), 11 (vertical gradient), 18 (horizontal gradient), 13 (isostatic gravity anomaly)

physical_signature: Sato-style ridge/valley lines of the second derivative (laplacian-of-gaussian) of the isostatic gravity anomaly - i.e. curvature, not gradient magnitude

why_it_should_catch_a_missing_fault: Gravity senses density contrast through basin fill, so it can express a fault that has NO surface scarp at all. The Gabbs Valley blind-system study lists 'intersecting and terminating gravity gradients, suggesting a complex zone of intersecting northwest- and north-northeast-striking faults' as one of five collocated indicators of a >130 C blind system in this province.

how_it_differs_from_prior_work: Sibling repositories ran curvature/tilt transforms on the MAGNETIC family (band 6 'tilt angle or total curvature') and on detrended elevation (scarp filters). The gravity family was used as gradients, not as a curvature/ridge-line transform.

cost: low (bands already on disk)

expected: medium

viability: No new data required for the gravity bands IF training_features.tif is placed (see the README limitation). The bands are not mirrored anywhere reachable from this sandbox.

8 — Structural position AND independent thermal manifestation (conjunction)

id: H41-I

rank: 4

layers: H41-A corridor geometry x gdr_wellspring_in_footprint.csv (temperature and three geothermometer estimates) x lidar_scarp_features_u8.tif

physical_signature: Emit only where a structural element and a measured thermal anomaly coincide - a conjunction, not a sum

why_it_should_catch_a_missing_fault: It inherits the physical evidence instead of the catalogue: a spring whose geothermometers exceed 150 C must be fed by a permeable path, and if that path is not mapped the dotted trace still earns credit.

how_it_differs_from_prior_work: Sibling work used thermal data as a smooth field to be emitted (H19-4/H19-5 'openness-thermal-pop'). Here it is a GATE on structural geometry, and the gate's discriminating power is measured by the site-control instrument in this repository.

cost: low (both tables already on disk)

expected: medium; mainly a precision instrument, so its natural mass is small

viability: No new data required.
