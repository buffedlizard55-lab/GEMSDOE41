RESEARCH NOTE / H41-A

A structural hypothesis.
Not a score promise.

The target is missing geological faults indicative of geothermal resources—not verified geothermal vents, reservoirs, or drill-ready prospects.

Why H33’s reported 0.2778 is plausible

Measured artifact and source-code evidence: GEMSDOE32 H33-2-B2 contains 37,654 binary dots. Its published method removes 2,545 pixels (6.33%) within 200 m of the catalogue from a 40,199-dot base reported at 0.2708. This is a precision/budget modification to an existing detector, not a new geological detector. Inspect the source site.

Mathematical explanation: the official metric rewards the maximum kernel-weighted prediction at each truth pixel; nearby redundant dots can add false-positive mass without adding coverage. Let T be weighted true positives, S prediction mass, M the sum of prediction times nearest-truth kernel, and G the number of truth pixels:

DTI = T / [0.2(T + S − M) + 0.8G]

If removed pixels contribute little unique coverage or truth proximity, reducing S improves this ratio. The hidden test faults are explicitly outside the existing catalogue, which makes near-catalogue pruning a reasonable initial-round hypothesis. It can still remove real adjacent new faults and may sacrifice final-round coverage.

Not established: the hidden labels and organizer artifact receipt are unavailable. The 0.2778 filename-to-score link is user-reported; the official leaderboard displays that score for a participant, not that file. H33’s own page still says unscored. Its “live mirror” uses SGMC proxy truth and prevalence calibration; it is not an independent held-out expert dataset and cannot validate a new geometry-only regime.

Can we score higher? Possibly, but neither prior scores nor this experiment establish that we can. A 0.2778→0.3262 change would be a 17.4% increase in DTI; it is not automatically a 17.4% increase in recall. Thresholds and synthetic truth models cannot manufacture new structural evidence.

Exact implementation

  1. Read the official INGENIOUS v2 archive and project full source geometry into EPSG:32611. Retain 5,540 trace parts from 413 source features inside the footprint. All 60,988 catalogue pixels lie within 300 m of this geometry.
  2. Compute length-weighted axial circular mean strike. Assign each trace to the nearer of 135° (NW) and 10° (N/NNE). Keep every trace in the CSV. Only parts ≥600 m, within 30° of their prototype, and axial concentration ≥0.4 can anchor corridors.
  3. For each NW endpoint, find the nearest N/NNE trace using exact line geometry. Maximum gap 5 km, outward tangent agreement ≥0.5 cosine beyond 200 m. Reject endpoints within 200 m of another NW part and within 300 m of the footprint edge.
  4. Score distance to the connector with a 500 m Gaussian (truncate at 1,500 m), a 5 km gap taper, and a sinusoidal along-connector taper. Use the maximum over accepted pairs. Suppress all cells within 200 m of the raster catalogue.
  5. Use only detrended elevation band 12 for local tangent orientation: 1-pixel smoothing, gradient structure tensor smoothed at 2 pixels, coherence ≥0.4, non-flat relief, and an 8-pixel valid-data erosion. This is a broad topographic lineament proxy, not a verified scarp detector.
  6. Find the nearer family’s rasterized geometry. Multiply corridor score by cos⁸ of the axial tangent mismatch and the detection mask. The factors are already bounded in [0,1]; no label-based normalization or arbitrary display rescaling is applied.
p(x) = max_corridors C(x) × cos⁸[Δstrike(x, closer family)] × detected(x)
C(x) = exp[−d²/(2·500²)] × exp[−gap/5000] × [0.35 + 0.65 sin(πu)]

All distances are metres; u is the clipped connector projection. All settings were fixed before evaluation. Input geophysical bands other than topography are never used.

Gate decision: CLOSED.

The model relies on nearby visible anchors that disappear under whole-trace blocked withholding. This reveals a support/generalization failure of the current detector–validation combination. It does not prove absence of geological transfer corridors, and it does not justify claiming a high leaderboard score. Do not shrink blocks after seeing this result merely to obtain a better number.

Scientific corrections and irregularities

  • Astor ≠ Emerson. Siler, Mayhew & Faulds studied Astor Pass: NW dextral / northerly normal fault interactions. Anderson & Faulds studied Emerson Pass: intersecting oppositely dipping normal faults in a regional displacement-transfer setting. Primary papers and citations.
  • Strike ≠ slip sense. Of 1,501 NW-assigned parts, source attributes report 724 RL, 686 normal, 90 LL, and one unspecified. Of 4,039 N/NNE-assigned parts, 3,129 are normal, 641 RL and 269 LL. These are geometric families, not verified kinematic labels. A forced two-population interpretation misses the ENE sinistral population described by Faulds et al.
  • The template mirror is not blank. It contains 60,988 ones coincident with labels, unlike the public page’s “total fault absence” wording. Only its grid and finite mask are used.
  • The repository was empty. Prior claims of a ready GPU train/inference pipeline and existing holdout did not describe this checkout.
  • Acquisition is mixed provenance. Official vector bytes were directly fetched by Actions; feature and label rasters are pinned owner mirrors, not independently authenticated against the login-walled originals.
  • Leaderboard correction. The official page showed 0.3262 on 2026-10-05, above the brief’s stale 0.3195. Owner scores and publicly displayed participant scores do not establish a filename receipt.

Next-session priorities

  1. Validation first: obtain or reconstruct clean historical-best out-of-fold predictions with the same whole-source exclusion policy. Keep the untouched 20 km audit set; preregister a separate transfer-zone test where donor and receiver faults remain visible and an intervening trace is withheld, with geographic grouping and a buffer preventing target leakage.
  2. Kinematic quality: test the source slip-sense attributes already acquired to resolve normal NW traces and ENE sinistral faults. Compare strike-only vs kinematics-constrained anchors on the new preregistered test—not the exhausted audit folds.
  3. Lineament specificity: use independent terrain scarps rather than broad contour coherence. USGS 3DEP high-resolution terrain is an official possible source; tile-level acquisition and license/provenance must be verified before treating it as available. Do not infer permeable vents from topography alone.
  4. Only then: test the H41-B convergence motif and H41-D topology stability. Rebuild every field from raw geometry, compare at honest prediction budgets, retain negative results, and spend a weekly slot only after a valid gate passes.

These are recommendations, not implemented successes or projected scores. No hidden labels or private test performance are available.
